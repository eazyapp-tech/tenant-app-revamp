# The note to Srijan after the 7 September review

From Sanchay to Srijan, 9 September 2026. Approved and sent the same day. Filed here so the commitments in it are a document and not a memory of a conversation.

Four calls were made and approved before it went. The critique of the tenant-type screen stays out of the note and is signalled rather than argued, so a repair does not turn into a debate. The process failure is named once, hard, and not repeated. The adoption number goes in, because it moves the conversation from taste to stakes. And the pack goes to him as files three days before the session, never as a link in the note itself, because a link in a repair message reads as homework.

---

Srijan,

Let me say your instruction back, so you can tell me where I have it wrong. That is what you asked Nitish for and never got, and it should have come from me anyway.

**The order you gave:** research insights first, from real calls. Then hypotheses. Then the audience types, with what is common and what differs between them, and the differences assigned to your four levers. Then we sit together and read it. Then scope. Then structure. Then theme. Design last, and in pieces.

**The thing you said three times and never named, "X".** My read is: define the audience types and lock the feature and flow structure with you, before anyone opens Figma, and that you gave that a month ago. If that is not what you meant, tell me now, because everything below is built on it.

**Four gaps, and they are real.**

1. **No insight ledger.** There is no list of what we learned, in tenants' own words, tied to named calls. This is the one you asked for first and it is the one that was missing.
2. **No structure lock.** No sitemap, no tab set, no mark on each screen saying whether it is a design problem or a flow problem. This is the biggest hole and it is on our side, not Nitish's.
3. **We never sat and read anything together.** Not once.
4. **Nothing has your signature on it.** Scope is locked for two of the three apps and not for the white-label one, and you have signed off on none of it.

**The part that is my fault, and is not what you think it is.** There is a written track behind this. Vision, brief, design language, what each module must become, the persuasion layer, and feature maps for two of the three apps. Thirteen documents. Nitish has had access since August. I never walked him or you through any of it, so a design track and a product track ran for a month without touching. That is the actual failure here, and it is mine.

**What is already moving.**

- The research kit is finished and runnable. Seventeen tenant interviews, owners, and the internal team, across Delhi NCR and Bengaluru, on named properties chosen from our own data rather than from memory.
- Internal sessions start this week. You will have the first ledger rows in your hand before we meet.
- Nitish has a written scope: the ledger, the audience types from evidence, and the visual brief per type. Everything else on your rungs one to three and five he reads, not rebuilds.

**One number, so you know what we are actually fixing.** Across four hundred thousand tenants, six to nine in a hundred open the app in a month. Whole properties with two hundred tenants have never had a single install. That is the problem. Not the screens.

**The ask.** Half a day with you on the 15th or 16th. You get the pack three days before, including a first cut of the structure: the sitemap, the tab set, and every screen marked as either a design problem or a flow problem. Read it cold and arrive annoyed at the right things. We go through it, you mark what is wrong, and structure locks in that room.

I have one disagreement with the tenant-type screen you sketched, and I am holding it for that room rather than putting it in writing here.

Sanchay
