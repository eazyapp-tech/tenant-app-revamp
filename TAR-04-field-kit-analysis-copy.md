# TAR-04 · The Field Kit · Analysis Copy

**This copy never goes into a room.** It holds what the study is for, the thirteen things we think we will find, which question feeds which, and what happens to the answers once the sessions are done. Read it once before the first session and again before you write anything up.

The questions themselves live in the Room Copy. They are numbered there, and the table in section M below is the only place the question numbers, the hypotheses and the outputs are joined up. If a question is renumbered in the Room Copy, fix it here in the same edit or the two copies come apart.

## What this round covers

Round one is **the standard RentOk tenant app (door 1)**, because that is the app being designed and the structure being locked. It carries a named **white-label slice (door 2)**: four tenants living in properties that run the app under their own name, one white-label owner, and the brand questions in section B4 of the Room Copy. **Open for all (door 3) is deferred to round two**, because it needs renters recruited outside the owner channel. Two questions reach into door 3 on purpose. They are marked in the table in section M, their answers are tallied on their own, and they do not shape any door 1 decision. They are asked anyway, because the record that becomes door 3's product is built by door 1 tenants.

### The hypotheses these questions test

Shape: we believe [who] [does what] because [what we already hold]. Right if [signal]. Eleven opened this kit and one of them was struck. Three more were added later. Thirteen are live.

- **H1 The axis.** Payer (self, parent, company) and length of stay separate needs more than the label student, professional, family. Right if ledger insights cluster by payer and duration.
- **H2 Why WhatsApp wins.** A human replies. Right if the first move on the day something broke is a person, not a form.
- **H3 Adoption dies at the handover.** Day one with the owner decides installs. Right if never-installed tenants describe no handover moment and pushing owners describe a ritual.
- **H4 Trust before adoption.** One wrong receipt or stale due ends usage. Right if lapsed users name the moment.
- **H5 The deposit is the emotional peak.** Right if the deposit comes up unprompted in leaving, and in what they would tell a friend.
- **H6 Daily loops differ by type.** Students daily for food and the gate, professionals monthly for rent, families for complaints and papers. Right if yesterday-evening recalls split that way.
- **H7 The comfort line sits at gender and family.** City and property are welcome; gender and family reporting are rejected. Right if concept-test rejections pattern by gender context.
- **H8 The payer who is not the tenant is unserved.** Right if owners report parent calls and parents describe workarounds.
- **H9 Owners push when a task leaves their plate.** Right if pushing owners name a removed task.
- **H10 The rewards carnival is invisible.** Right if reliability beats cashback in the deck and nobody recalls a reward.
- **H11 The property's name buys trust that RentOk's does not.** A tenant in a white-label property trusts an app carrying their property's name more than the same app carrying ours, and neither knows nor cares that RentOk is underneath. Right if white-label tenants name the property as the app's owner and say the RentOk version would feel the same or worse. This is the only tenant-facing test of whether a repainted app is read as the property's own. It does not test the rest of our fourth law, which says that our typeface, our motion and our voice survive the repaint. Nothing in this kit tests that, and no theme should be locked as though it did. (Door 2 patch.)
- **H12 The tenant would recommend us to their own landlord, and has never been asked.** The block is not willingness, it is that nobody ever suggested it. Right if tenants say yes readily and name no reason beyond never having thought of it. This is the only path that reaches a paying customer directly, and nobody has ever asked it. Two invite screens exist in the app today and each is broken in a different way. The one built into the joining screen is switched off for every white-label tenant, though its button does work. A second one, in the account sheet, everybody can see and its button does nothing at all. (Five-question patch.)
- **H13 A visible trade changes how a burden feels.** A feature the property needs and the tenant did not ask for is accepted when what the tenant gets back is shown at the same moment. Attendance is the case. Right if the same feature, shown with its return, moves people who rejected it plain. This is TAR-05's exchange rule, and nothing has tested it. (Five-question patch.)
- ~~**H0 Tenants will hesitate to join a property without the app.**~~ Struck. The north star; nothing in September can test it. Its baseline comes from Q8, in A1 Arriving, which asks whether anything on a phone entered the decision at all. Without that number we will never know the needle moved.

### Why the Room Copy carries no tags, and why nothing is tagged by tenant type

Both absences are deliberate. If you change them, the study breaks.

**No tags in the room.** The hypothesis a question tests and the output it feeds change nothing about how the question is asked. They are our bookkeeping, so they live here, in the table in section M, and not on the page Nitish reads aloud from. Only the four things that change his behaviour stay on that page: ask about now, let them imagine, make them choose, and never drop this one.

**Door lives at section level, not on each question.** The white-label questions (A-W) and the owner's brand section (B4) are door 2. Two questions are door 3 and are marked as such in section M. Everything else is door 1, which is why nothing is marked door 1: a mark on a hundred questions carries no information.

**Nothing is tagged by tenant type, and nothing should be.** Every tenant gets the same questions. That is what makes the answers comparable, and comparability is the only way output 3 can derive the types from evidence instead of assuming them. Tagging a question "for students" would decide in advance the very thing H1 says we do not know: whether student, professional and family are the real dividing lines at all, or whether who pays and how long they stay matter more.

## What happens to the answers

- Every ledger row ends up carrying a hypothesis tag and an output tag. Nitish leaves those two columns blank in the field, because the tags are not on the copy he carries; they are added at the desk from the table in section M. After round one each hypothesis is marked **supported, challenged, or open**, with the I-numbers that decided it. Struck ones stay struck and visible.
- **O3:** cluster the ledger by type, by payer, by length of stay, and by age. Report which axis actually separates the rows. Then set that against four types (Srijan), five (TAR-01) and six (Nitish's list) and state a number with its evidence.
- **O4:** one page per type, in workflow words only, every sentence citing an I-number, a diary entry or a shadow-visit line.
- **O5:** Nitish writes the visual brief per type from the life-texture answers (A6.7) and the diary photographs. That instruction now also sits in the Room Copy beside the diary, where he will actually meet it. Our only check is that every line points at a session. A line with no source is struck.
- **O6:** needs ranked per type from A6.5, A7 and the deck tallies across tenants, owners and internal. A build candidate is strong on tenant picks, owner pushes and internal heard-demand together. Anything strong only in our own enthusiasm goes to the **no evidence yet** list, visibly.
- **H0's baseline:** count how many tenants named anything digital in Q8, in A1 Arriving. That number is the line the north star is measured from later.
- **Door 2:** A-W answers and B4 are read together and reported as their own short section, never folded into door 1's findings. H11 is marked on that section alone. If the answers to W1 name the property rather than RentOk, the commercial premise of our fourth law holds: a repainted app is taken as the property's own. That is one part of the law and not the whole of it, so it clears one gate before the theme locks, not all of them. If they name RentOk, or shrug, the law needs rethinking before anything is locked. Q51 in A8 asks every tenant the same forced choice and is read alongside these.
- **Door 3:** the two questions marked door 3 in section M are tallied on their own and carried to round two. They do not shape any door 1 decision.
- **Data-side tests, run in parallel by us.** Every one of these was tried against our own records on 8 September before being written here, because a test nobody has run is a promise, not a plan. Four of the seven did not survive that. What is left:
  - **H1, how long people stay.** Runnable and rich. We hold a stay record for about a million people.
  - **H3, do properties that use the joining flow get more installs.** Runnable, but not as a property-level test. No column records whether a property actually uses the joining flow. An invite link has been generated for about three properties in ten, which records only that somebody made a link. What we do hold is a marker on the individual tenant saying they came through onboarding, set for about twenty-four tenants in every hundred. So the test has to be run tenant by tenant and then rolled up, and it will speak for a quarter of our tenants, not all of them. A second field, saying where the lead came from, is filled for only about four in a hundred and is a different question altogether.
  - **H5, how long a deposit takes to come back.** Runnable, on a smaller sample than it first looks. We hold sixty-seven thousand refunds, all dated, but most are refunded credits and other things. About eighteen thousand of them are worded as a deposit or a move-out refund. The wording is typed by hand, so treat eighteen thousand as the rough size and separate it properly before reporting a number of days.
  - **H6, how the daily loop differs.** Runnable, and it already says something. Attendance was marked at three hundred and fourteen properties in the last three months, by nine thousand people, which is about two in every hundred tenants we have. Those nine thousand did not mark it every day, but they did mark it on roughly three quarters of the days between their own first mark and their last. So the picture is not that nobody uses the app; it is that where one thing is required, that one thing becomes close to a habit for the few who do it. That belongs in the reading of H13 as much as H6. Before it is used, confirm who is actually doing the marking, the tenant or the staff, because our records are ambiguous on that and the whole meaning turns on it. Instrument B asks an owner directly. Food is thinner but not empty: about three thousand tenants have confirmed a meal, against sixteen thousand confirmations in total.
  - **H10, do people take the rewards.** Runnable, but only as a plain count, not a rate. We record a reward when it is taken and not when it is offered, so there is no denominator. Ten thousand rewards have ever been taken, against four hundred thousand current tenants.
  - **H8, the payer who is not the tenant. NOT RUNNABLE, and this is a finding in itself.** The link between a tenant and the family member or company paying for them is recorded zero times across all four hundred and nine thousand current tenants, and ten times in the whole table of one and a half million. It is not sparse, it is unused. H8 is therefore an interview question and nothing else, and owners are the only way to reach a parent. That the link has never once been filled in is itself the strongest thing we can say about how well this person is served today.
  - **H12, has anyone ever seen the invite-your-landlord screen. NOT RUNNABLE.** Nothing records that a screen was seen. What the app itself shows is worse than not counting: there are two invite screens and each fails differently. The one in the joining screen is hidden from every white-label tenant, though its button works. The one in the account sheet is visible to everyone and its button does nothing. Both were checked in the code on 8 September. There is nothing to count and no need to count it.
  - Standing rule for anything added here later: run it once before writing it down.
- TAR-03's "our read" claims get marked supported, challenged or open. TAR-01's bets get the same. The joint session reads those marks.

## How this kit got here
- v3 draft, 8 Sep 2026: re-aimed at Srijan's six outputs; ten hypotheses with H0 struck; the capture sheet made the ledger; recording rule; never-installed and payer variants; Nitish's Friendzo walk kept as the owner spine; life-texture block for the visual brief; diary and shadow visit; two rules added.
- Door 2 patch: scope declared in the header; H11 added; two or three white-label tenants and variant A-W; the white-label owner made a named slot; two questions added to B4; the two door 3 questions tagged.
- Five-question patch and English pass: H12 and H13 added. Five questions added, one per hole found by auditing the kit against the vision documents rather than against itself. Q8 gives the struck north star a baseline. Q58 asks the tenant-to-their-own-landlord vector, our largest commercial path, never once asked. B15 asks owners what they would switch off, which is the switchboard. Q56 and B27 test TAR-05's exchange rule with attendance as the case. Q54 tests whether the WhatsApp thread is wanted inside the app. Hinglish removed throughout: the kit now states intent in English, and the spoken script per language is a named gate before any real session, written with someone who speaks it. Must-ask questions marked so a short session still produces comparable rows.
- Systems audit and nine fixes, 8 Sep 2026, after Sanchay asked whether a reader would know which door, person and type a question belongs to. The tagging turned out to be right and unexplained, and the audit found worse. Fixed: the tenant instrument's sections summed to 75 minutes against a header claiming 45, so three honest lengths are now defined at 70, 50 and 22 with the cuts named in advance; the capture sheet now records door, instrument, variants and session length, without which the contract's separate door 2 report is impossible; the sixteen cards are printed at A8.5 instead of promised in an appendix, so the kit is runnable; a routing table says who gets which instrument; the absence of door and persona tags is now explained as deliberate, with the reason; a list of what the interviewer does not do, aimed at the specific risk that the person asking designed the app; a worked ledger row; a mechanism for guardian consent; a stopping rule; and cross-references now name their section so renumbering cannot break them. Compensation ruled by Sanchay: three hundred rupees per tenant session, two hundred more for the diary, nothing for owners or internal.
- Section 1 filled, the analysis contract corrected, and an adversarial fact and readability pass run and applied, 8 Sep 2026. That pass found six wrong facts and nine misleading ones, almost all of them in the material written that same day, and every one is corrected above. The lesson is written into the contract: run a test before writing down what it will show. Cities ruled: Delhi NCR and Bengaluru. Thirteen slots, filled by fifteen named properties and twenty-two in total once the Blue Bells group is counted out, all chosen against our own records rather than from memory, each carrying the share of its tenants whose app has contacted us in the last sixty days, which is the reason it was picked. The white-label tenant slot went from two or three to four, split between one property where the app is used every day and one where it has never been opened, so that what we learn about a property's own name does not come only from places that are going well. Three recruiting warnings added: rent figures are wrong on properties billed by the term, families in flats barely exist in our NCR properties, and the four never-installed sessions rest entirely on three owners agreeing to hand over names of people who do not use the app. The seven data-side tests were then run for the first time instead of being assumed, and two of them turned out to be impossible while three more had to be narrowed. The link between a tenant and whoever pays for them is recorded zero times out of four hundred and nine thousand, so H8 is now interview-only. Nothing records that a screen was seen, so H12 cannot be counted. The joining-flow test has no property-level marker and has to be run tenant by tenant. The deposit refund sample is about eighteen thousand, not the sixty-seven thousand refunds we hold in total. Rewards are recorded when taken and never when offered, so there is no rate to report. What the surviving tests turned up is in the contract, including the finding that attendance is marked by about two tenants in a hundred and that those two mark it on roughly three quarters of their own days. Revised down from an initial five hundred after noticing that a flat amount reads differently across registers: five hundred is close to a day's wage in the cash-first and blue-collar slots, which makes it coercive rather than courteous. Kept flat rather than scaled, never mentioned at recruitment, handed over unconditionally at the end.
